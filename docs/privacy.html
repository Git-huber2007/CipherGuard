Privacy Policy & Zero-Payload Architecture

Effective Date: September 2026 · Version 2.4 · Custody Classification: Sovereign / Restricted

1. The Epistemological Zero-Payload Boundary

CipherGuard operates on a foundational architectural principle: Passive Wire Inspection with Zero Payload Decryption. When listening on a network mirror port or TAP device, our packet dissector parses cleartext protocol headers (such as IKE_SA_INIT Security Associations, SPI identifiers, and RFC 4303 Sequence Numbers) but never captures, decrypts, or persists payload bytes.

Cryptographic Guarantees: Everything on the wire that is encrypted stays encrypted. CipherGuard deduces cryptographic suite health, algorithm parameters, and padding anomalies purely from statistical framing analysis without touching decryption keys.

2. Local Storage and Sovereign Host Retention

CipherGuard is designed for air-gapped, sovereign defense deployment. Unlike cloud-connected security dashboards:

  • No External Telemetry Egress: Assessment results, PCAP file hashes, and RF scans remain strictly inside your designated host filesystem and local SQLite database (cipherguard-plans.db).
  • No Third-Party Analytics: No cookies, analytics trackers, or external CDN beacons are loaded from third-party tracking services. All fonts and style assets are packaged locally or loaded with strict privacy headers.
  • Client-Side Storage: Your browser’s localStorage is used strictly for operational interface states (active tab selection, theme configuration, and baseline comparison filters).

3. RF Spectrogram & Wireless Audit Custody

The Live Wi-Fi inspection module samples 802.11 beacon frames and probe responses directly from the local host wireless adapter. Signal strengths (RSSI), BSSID identifiers, authentication suites (WPA2/WPA3), and detected rogue AP parameters are processed in transient memory to detect Evil Twin attacks and are never transmitted to external cloud endpoints.

4. Statutory & Regulatory Alignment

CipherGuard adheres strictly to statutory privacy guidelines:

  • Information Technology Act, 2000 (India): Compliance with Sections 43A and 72A safeguarding confidential electronic communications.
  • ISO/IEC 27001 (Control A.8.24): Use of cryptography and key custody management.
  • NIST SP 800-77 Rev. 1: Guide to IPsec VPNs security baseline definitions.
  • GDPR Article 5(1)(c) (Data Minimisation): Only strictly necessary packet header metadata is evaluated.
